Chassis Assembly and Parts List

Section focus: Robotic Vehicle — Assembly & Competition Prep — Lesson 4.4.1  |  Duration: ~50 minutes

Builds on: every earlier Section 4 lesson. By this point, all subsystems (sensors, motors, decision logic) have been tested individually — this lesson is about combining them into one physical vehicle.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, you will be able to:

  • Identify every part needed for a complete 2-wheel line-following vehicle
  • Assemble the chassis, motors, and sensor array into one physical unit
  • Route and secure wiring so it survives movement without disconnecting
  • Position the sensor array and power sources sensibly on the finished chassis

Check quantities before you start. Individual component kits are often bought as one shared classroom set — confirm your specific group actually has two DC motors, and enough IR sensor pairs for your chosen array size, before assembly day. Running short partway through is far more disruptive than checking in advance.

2. Assembly Order

1Mount the motors and wheels

Attach both DC motors to the chassis plate according to your chassis kit's instructions, then attach the wheels. Confirm both wheels spin freely by hand before adding any electronics.

2Attach the caster wheel

Mount the caster (or ball caster) at the opposite end from the main drive wheels, providing the third point of contact for balance.

3Mount the sensor array

Position your IR sensor array (Lesson 4.1.2) at the front of the chassis, facing down, at the height determined during calibration (Lesson 4.1.4). Secure it firmly — a sensor array that shifts position after calibration will need recalibrating.

4Mount the Arduino, driver, and battery pack

Position these toward the rear/centre of the chassis for balance, keeping the motor battery pack and driver wiring separate from the Arduino's own power where possible, consistent with Lesson 3.11's power isolation principle.

5Wire everything together

Connect the sensor array, motor driver, and any optional modules (RGB LED, LCD) to the Arduino, following your tested wiring from the individual subsystem lessons. Double-check every connection against your working breadboard version before assuming the assembled version will behave identically.

6Secure and route wiring

Use cable ties or tape to keep wires away from the wheels and caster, where they could snag or be pulled loose during movement. A wire that worked fine sitting still on a desk can easily disconnect once the vehicle is actually driving around.

Test after every step, not just at the end. If something stops working partway through assembly, you'll have a far easier time finding the cause if you tested the previous step first, rather than discovering a problem only once everything is fully assembled.
Try it yourself:
  1. Confirm you have every item on the parts list before starting assembly.
  2. Assemble the chassis and motors, and confirm both wheels spin freely.
  3. Mount and wire the sensor array, and re-run a quick sensor test (Lesson 4.1.2's code) before moving on.
  4. Challenge: once fully assembled, re-run your full calibration process (Lesson 4.1.4) from scratch — mounting height and angle may have changed slightly from your test-bench setup, and thresholds should reflect the vehicle's actual final configuration.
