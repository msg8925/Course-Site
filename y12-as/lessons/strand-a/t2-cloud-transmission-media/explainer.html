01

What is cloud computing?

Cloud computing means storing data and running services on servers owned and maintained by someone else, accessed over the internet, rather than on hardware you own and manage locally. When you save a file to Google Drive or Dropbox instead of your own hard disk, you're using cloud storage.

Cloud vs local, in one sentence Local storage is a disk you own and are physically responsible for; cloud storage is disk space someone else owns, that you rent access to over a network connection.
02

Public vs private cloud

Public cloudPrivate cloud
Infrastructure shared byMany different customers, on the same provider's hardwareOne organisation only, on dedicated hardware
Typical costLower — pay only for what you use, no hardware to buyHigher — dedicated infrastructure costs more to build and run
Control over dataLess — the provider's policies and infrastructure choices applyMore — the organisation sets its own security and access policies
Neither is simply "better" Public cloud's low cost and easy scalability suit organisations without special data-sensitivity requirements. Private cloud's extra control suits organisations — hospitals, banks, government bodies — with strict legal or security requirements over exactly where their data lives and who can access it. The right choice depends entirely on the situation.

04

Wired media: copper vs fibre-optic

Copper cableFibre-optic cable
Carries data asElectrical signalsPulses of light
Speed over distanceSignal weakens (attenuates) faster over distanceMaintains speed over far longer distances
InterferenceSusceptible to electromagnetic interferenceImmune to electromagnetic interference
CostCheaper to buy and installMore expensive, especially to install

Fibre-optic's combination of speed, distance, and immunity to interference is why it's used for the backbone connections between cities and countries, even though copper remains cheaper for short local runs.

Wireless media: radio, microwave, satellite

MediumTypical useKey limitation
Radio waves / WiFiLocal wireless networking — phones, laptops, home routersLimited range; walls and other signals cause interference
MicrowavesPoint-to-point links, e.g. between two nearby buildingsRequires a clear, uninterrupted line of sight between transmitter and receiver
SatellitesCoverage of remote areas with no other infrastructure nearbySignals travel a very long distance, adding noticeable delay (latency)
